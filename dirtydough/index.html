Dirty Dough Franchise

Karl Sweets LLC · 14123-B Baltimore Avenue, Laurel, Maryland

In May 2023, I signed a franchise agreement and set out to bring a cookie shop to Laurel, Maryland. I built it from an empty insurance office into a working store, ran it with a team of more than ten people, and kept it open until May 2025.

Stuffed cookies were the whole idea, layers, fillings, and mix-ins hidden inside every cookie.

Why Laurel

I lived in College Park and worked in Bethesda, and first wanted to open closer to Washington, D.C. Licensing constraints pushed me to look elsewhere, and Laurel, with very few dessert options of its own, was the gap worth filling. After I secured the location, thank-you messages started coming in from the community.

“Laurel is almost the perfect town… everybody seems to know everybody. It’s an incredible community.” Brandon, in Hef Streams Media’s Climb Back Series, April 2025

The brand

Dirty Dough started in Tempe, Arizona, its first store opened in March 2020, and grew fast on franchising from late 2021. The product was a stuffed, three-layer cookie with a rotating weekly menu, and the message was that the best part is on the inside. In January 2024, Craveworthy Brands bought a significant stake in the chain, with its founder Gregg Majewski becoming Dirty Dough’s CEO; Craveworthy became the sole owner later that year. By the end of 2024 the chain had nearly 70 locations. In 2025 the brand went through a difficult stretch, and several locations closed that year, including mine.

The build

I signed the franchise agreement in May 2023, with Karl Sweets LLC, my Maryland company, as the franchisee, funded with SBA-backed financing, two 7(a) loans totaling $380,000 through Huntington National Bank, approved in July 2023, plus personal capital. The space was a corner unit in a small shopping center on Route 1: about 1,000 square feet that had been a State Farm insurance office. I ran the contractor bidding, permits, vendors, and opening day, and managed the buildout that turned it into a take-out cookie shop. In early August 2024, the OPEN sign went on.

The store

We sold stuffed three-layer cookies for about $5 each, in singles and multi-packs, with flavors that changed week to week, plus shakes and Dirty Drinks. Customers ordered in store and through DoorDash and Uber Eats. I owned the full P&L, end to end, and hired and led a team of more than ten people.

Customers rated the store 4.5 stars across 63 reviews, with 4.6 on Uber Eats and 4.7 on DoorDash. The words that kept coming up:

“surprise fillings in every bite” “rich, gooey center” “straight-up magic”

“The filling and the cookie is cooked so well and it’s overall delicious.” Ethan B., Uber Eats, October 2024

Fresh-baked, served warm, boxed to go.

In the community

Two months after opening, the store was featured in Voices of Laurel , Diane Mezzanotte’s review, “Dirty Dough Offers Decadent Stuffed Cookies,” called the cookies “HEAVEN.” In early 2025, I was featured in Hef Streams Media’s Climb Back Series , telling the story of building the business. Local creators stopped by too, one January 2025 visit called it “the dirtiest place in town.”

Timeline

Buildout: a former insurance office becomes a take-out cookie shop.

Open for business, in store, on DoorDash and Uber Eats, featured in local news and the Climb Back Series.

The store closes its doors for the last time.

Two years, one storefront. The community showed up for it: 4.5 stars across 63 reviews, and thank-you messages before the doors even opened.
