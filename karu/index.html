Käru Stroller System

A self-stabilizing stroller bassinet.

Project archive · 2020–2024 · Closed

Käru was a stroller insert that kept a baby level on any terrain. We designed it, engineered it, built a working prototype, and tested it — and closed the project in 2024 before it reached market. This page is the record of what we built.

The Käru bassinet, design render. The insert fits existing strollers and levels itself as the terrain changes.

The idea

Strollers bounce. On trails, cobblestones, and uneven sidewalks, every bump reaches the baby. Käru set out to fix that with a stroller insert that did the stabilizing itself — so parents could take their children on any adventure without the ride waking them up.

The answer was a dual-axis gimbal suspension system: the bassinet hangs in a frame that absorbs movement in real time, minimizing tilt and vibration as the stroller moves over rough ground. The final design used aluminum, technical plastics, and performance textiles — light, durable, and built for daily and outdoor use.

How it was built

  • 2020

    Founded

    Brandon Hoffmann and Fred Cary start Käru Strollers with one idea: a stroller with no limitations. The venture was developed in coordination with IdeaPros.

  • Concept

    Design with Lime Creative Design

    Market and user research, sketches, and mechanical studies. The geometry was optimized for balance and portability, and the dual-axis gimbal system took shape.

  • Engineering

    Built to hold steady

    Optimized weight distribution, finite element analysis and load simulations, and component-level decisions on springs, bearings, and materials — tuned to balance strength, weight, and motion control.

  • Prototype

    A working prototype

    Commercial strollers were reverse-engineered for reference dimensions. Custom components were 3D-printed, and a prototype bed was fitted to an existing frame to test the stabilization mechanisms. It worked.

  • Sourcing

    Ready for manufacturing

    Manufacturing partners in the U.S. and overseas were evaluated and factory quotations compared, to find the right factory for production.

  • 2024

    Closed

    Funding could not be solved, and the project closed before going to market. A provisional patent was filed, but no full patent was ever granted. The design remains with the founders.
